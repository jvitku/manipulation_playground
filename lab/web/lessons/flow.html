The task

The same two problems as the previous task, with the same scripted bimodal expert and the same data. T1: an obstacle at \(c \in [-0.4,\, 0.4]\), the policy outputs a 2-D waypoint that passes it on the left or on the right. T2: a closed-loop point mass from near (0, -1) to near (0, 1) around a disc of radius 0.3, with action chunks of horizon steps of which the first n_exec are played before re-planning. The only thing that changes is the head: instead of regressing one action, the network models the whole distribution of expert actions and the policy draws a sample from it.

The data

Nothing new — and that is the point. Both sides of the obstacle are equally good and equally represented, so the training set is bimodal at every c. MSE averaged those modes into a crash (100% of samples inside the obstacle in this repo's tutorial run). A generative head is trained on the same pairs and has no reason to average.

The method

Flow matching (the default head; the convention of pi0 / SmolVLA). Draw noise \(e \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)\) and a time \(t \in (0, 1]\), and put the expert chunk \(x_0\) and the noise on a straight line:

$$x_t = t\,e + (1 - t)\,x_0$$

\(t = 1\) is pure noise, \(t = 0\) is data.

The line has a constant velocity \(e - x_0\). The network sees \((x_t, t, \text{obs})\) and predicts a velocity \(v\); the loss compares the two:

$$L = \left\lVert v - (e - x_0) \right\rVert^2$$

Many different \((x_0, e)\) pairs pass through the same point \(x_t\); regression averages their velocities, which gives the marginal velocity field — a field that carries the noise distribution onto the data distribution. Sampling: start at \(x = e\) (pure noise) and take \(K\) Euler steps from \(t = 1\) to \(t = 0\):

$$x \leftarrow x - \frac{1}{K}\, v(x, t)$$

Each draw of noise ends on one mode or the other: the multimodality lives in the noise, not in the network output.

DDPM / DDIM (the alternative head, Diffusion Policy). A cosine schedule noises the chunk,

$$x_k = \sqrt{\bar\alpha_k}\, x_0 + \sqrt{1 - \bar\alpha_k}\, e$$

The network predicts the noise \(\hat\epsilon\):

$$L = \left\lVert \hat\epsilon - e \right\rVert^2$$

Sampling is deterministic DDIM over a subsequence of \(K\) of the 100 training timesteps. Same idea, curved path, different target.

The architecture

B = batch, H = action chunk (1 in T1, Action chunk in T2). The loss node sits on the comparison of the network's velocity with the line's velocity \(e - x_0\) — the interpolation is the only place the expert data enters. Compared to the previous task, the zeros fed to the net are replaced by the noisy chunk and the real time embedding.

Flow-matching loss: regress the velocity of a straight line
def loss(self, model, x0: torch.Tensor, cond, mask: torch.Tensor | None = None):
    # ...
    e = torch.randn_like(x0)
    t = self.sample_t(x0.shape[0], x0.device)
    tt = t[:, None, None]
    x_t = tt * e + (1 - tt) * x0
    v, extra = model(x_t, t, cond)
    err = (v - (e - x0)).pow(2).mean(-1)  # (B, H)
    # ...
    return err.mean(1), extra
Sampling: Euler steps from noise to an action chunk
def sample(self, model, shape, cond, steps: int = 10, generator=None, trace: bool = False):
    x = torch.randn(shape, generator=generator, device=_dev(cond, model))
    xs = [x]
    dt = 1.0 / steps
    for i in range(steps):
        t = torch.full((shape[0],), 1.0 - i * dt, device=x.device)
        v, _ = model(x, t, cond)
        x = x - dt * v
        if trace:
            xs.append(x)
    return (x, torch.stack(xs)) if trace else x

What to watch

Run it with the defaults (flow, K = 8) and read the T1 collision curve and the viewer. In this repo's tutorial (docs/ta_tutorial.md, 20k steps, lr 1e-3, 3 seeds) the flow head put 3.0% of T1 samples inside the obstacle against 100% for MSE, and DDPM 4.7%. The noise to actions animation shows why: the noise cloud is unimodal, and the learned field pulls each point toward the nearer mode, so the samples split into two branches that flank the shaded band instead of falling into it.

Then use the sampling-step sweep after an evaluation: with the tutorial's flow net the T1 collision rate was 99.95% / 73% / 19% / 5.3% / 1.3% / 0.85% for K = 1 / 2 / 4 / 8 / 16 / 32. One step integrates a bent field in a straight line and lands in the middle (the mean again!); the knee is around 8–16 steps, and past it extra steps buy little. DDIM behaves less smoothly (its tutorial T1 sweep peaks at 37.8% for K = 4), so do not read DDPM's sweep as a clean curve. In closed loop the gap is the other way round — tutorial T2 with H = 16: flow 99%, DDPM 67.5%, MSE 87% — so check the head in closed loop before trusting an open-loop number.

Knobs

  • Generative head: flow matching (velocity target, Euler sampler) or DDPM (noise target, DDIM sampler).
  • Sampling steps K: network evaluations per sampled chunk. Used by the periodic evaluations during training and the final scatter and rollouts; the sweep always covers 1–32. Cost grows linearly with K.
  • Demonstrations: expert episodes collected. More demos reduce noise in the field; they are not what separates the modes.
  • Training steps: optimiser steps per part. Generative heads need many more steps than regression to sharpen the modes (the tutorial saw the samples still blurred across the gap at 5k steps and lr 3e-4); the loss flattens long before the behaviour is right.
  • Learning rate: the default is 1e-3 (the tutorial's setting); at 3e-4 the modes separate several thousand steps later.
  • Batch size: samples per step; mostly a noise and speed knob.
  • Action chunk (T2) and Actions executed per chunk: how many actions are predicted, and how many of them are played open loop before a fresh sample is drawn (clamped to the chunk length). Executing fewer than the whole chunk keeps the policy reactive.
  • Expert noise: jitter on the demonstrated actions; blurs the modes the field has to resolve.
