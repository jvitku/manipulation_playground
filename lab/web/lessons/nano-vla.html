The question

Can a vision–language–action model built from scratch — every block written in this repo, 7.5 M parameters, trained in minutes on one GPU — act from pixels and words? And, the question the whole force-VLA school hangs on: what does such a model miss when it has no sense of touch? This lesson builds nanoVLA (vision stem, tiny language path, transformer trunk, flow-matching action expert), trains it on lesson 15's demonstrations, and lets the claims board answer with numbers: on the fully visible T0 it should be nearly perfect (claim c4), on T1 — where the click threshold is hidden from the camera — it should land mid-range by design (c5), and on the two-button T1 the instruction should be load-bearing (c6). The gap c5 leaves open is exactly the room lesson 17 fills with a force input.

The task

Both tasks, cameras and demos come from lesson 15's testbed. The policy sees two 96×96 cameras (static + wrist), the 8-D state [x, y, z, yaw, grip, vx, vy, vz] and one instruction from a 23-word vocabulary; it answers with a chunk of H = 16 actions (Δp xyz + gripper), of which the first n_exec = 8 are executed at 10 Hz before it re-plans. No wrench enters the model anywhere — that is lesson 17.

T1 is the designed blind spot: vision + words find and press the right button, but when the click comes and how hard is too hard leak only through force. A vision-only policy should therefore sit mid-range on T1 — the M1 gate expects 30–70%, and a result above the window means the task premise broke (re-randomise T1), not that the model is good.

The method

Prefix / suffix. nanoVLA is the π0 / SmolVLA shape at 1/50th scale: a prefix encodes what the world looks like and what was asked — [image ; text] through the trunk into \(E \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times S \times 256}\), S ≤ 84 tokens — and a suffix turns noise into actions by reading E through cross-attention. The split is what makes iterative sampling cheap: the prefix is computed once per decision, the small suffix runs K = 10 times for the Euler integration.

Vision → 72 tokens. One conv stem (4 stride-2 layers, 32–256 channels) is shared by both cameras: 96 → 48 → 24 → 12 → 6, so each image becomes a 6×6 grid of 256-D vectors = 36 tokens, 72 for the pair — [B, 72, 256]. A learned 2-D position embedding (row table + column table) says where a token sits in its image; a learned per-camera embedding says which camera it came from, which is all that tells the two apart since the stem weights are shared.

Text path. The vocabulary is the course's 23 words (every instruction word of T0–T3 plus the subtask words lesson 18 will need). Tokenize = lowercase, split, look up, pad to T = 12; <pad> positions are masked out of every attention read downstream. The embeddings pass one encoder layer → [B, ≤12, 256].

Trunk. Four pre-norm encoder layers (4 heads, FFN 768) run self-attention over the concatenated [image ; text] sequence — this is where "the red button" meets the red pixels. Output: the prefix E and its key mask. The trunk is nanoVLA's "VLM" — and deliberately stays force-free in every later lesson (both papers found force must not enter the VLM pathway).

Action expert. The suffix is [state token ; 16 noisy action tokens]: the 8-D state through a linear lift, each noisy action \(x_\tau[t]\) through a linear lift plus the flow-time embedding \(\tau\) (log-spaced sin/cos through a 2-layer MLP, the same τ for the whole chunk) plus a learned suffix position. Four decoder layers run causal self-attention over the suffix (an action token reads the state and earlier steps, never later ones) and cross-attention into E (masked where E is <pad>); a linear head turns the 16 chunk features into the velocity [B, 16, 4].

Flow matching — the same object lessons 2 and 4 taught, now conditioned on pixels and words. Actions are scaled to model units in [−1, 1] (dp / 0.05 m, gripper × 2 − 1; fixed, nothing fitted). Draw \(\tau \sim \mathrm{Beta}(1.5, 1)\), noise \(e \sim \mathcal N(0, I)\), interpolate on the straight line and regress its velocity:

$$x_\tau = \tau\, e + (1 - \tau)\, x_0, \qquad u = \frac{d x_\tau}{d \tau} = e - x_0, \qquad \mathcal L = \lVert v_\theta(x_\tau, \tau \mid E, \text{state}) - u \rVert^2,$$

masked where the chunk runs past the episode end (the dataset's pad_mask). At inference, start at pure noise (\(\tau = 1\)) and integrate to data (\(\tau = 0\)) with K Euler steps:

$$x \leftarrow x - \tfrac{1}{K}\, v_\theta(x, \tau), \qquad K = 10.$$

Chunking. Predict H = 16 steps, execute n_exec = 8, re-plan — lesson 4's trade-off verbatim: longer execution is smoother and cheaper but reacts a whole chunk late; executing all 16 open-loop on T1 is this lesson's deliberately-wrong exercise. Training: AdamW (lr 3e-4, linear warmup then cosine to 0), grad-clip 1.0, batch 64, and an EMA copy (decay 0.999) that is what acts and what the checkpoint evaluates — the house convention.

The no-language arm. The claims suite's arm C trains and evaluates the identical config on T1 with every instruction replaced by a constant <unk> token — text that exists but says nothing. (Not the all-<pad> text: with every key masked, the text encoder's self-attention softmax is NaN. It is an arm flag in the task wrapper, not a model change; the env still draws its real target button.)

Motivation — why each choice is in this experiment

From scratch at d = 256, not a pretrained VLM. A real VLA inherits a web-scale VLM because its scenes are the open world. These scenes are 96×96 renders of one gantry and the whole language is 23 words — there is no web knowledge to inherit, and a from-scratch 7.5 M-parameter model trains in ~11 minutes on the 3080, which is what makes the lesson-17/18 ablation suites affordable. The price is honest too: every block is ~100 lines the lesson can quote, instead of a frozen black box.

Why a flow head and not regression. Lesson 2's collapse: an MSE head averages modes. T1 always shows two buttons; half the demos go left, half right. Conditioned on the instruction the target is (mostly) unimodal, but the starting approach, the press depth and T0's grasp timing are genuinely multimodal — a generative head samples a coherent chunk instead of the mean of several. The flow unit test of milestone M1 pins the other direction: with one data mode the field is nearly straight and even one Euler step lands on the mean.

Why EMA. The flow loss is noisy (fresh e and τ every step), so the raw weights jitter; the EMA shadow is a cheap average that evaluates several points better and is the lab's checkpoint convention anyway (lab/core/loop.py) — the optimizer never sees it.

Why 20 k steps. The loss is near its plateau well before (first drop is ~10× within 2 k steps); 20 k at the measured 22.9 steps/s is ~15 min — inside the M1 gate's "20 k < 20 min" budget, with the cosine tail giving the EMA a quiet run-out. The recorded suite run used 15 k per arm so three arms fit one evening; the shortened choice is written into its run record.

Why T1's expected 30–70% window IS the design. The course needs headroom a force input can claim in lesson 17. If vision alone solved T1, the threshold would not be hidden (premise broken → re-randomise); if it scored ~0%, the task would be too hard to show a clean force-vs-no-force gap. c5 is a claim about the course, not about the model — and the board treats leaving the window above as NOT CONFIRMED with an explicit re-randomise note.

Theory — the VLA recipe, and what the papers would say

The recipe. A modern VLA is: a vision encoder into tokens, language into tokens, one transformer over both (the "VLM"), and an action expert — a second, smaller transformer that cross-attends into the VLM's output and generates an action chunk with a diffusion/flow objective. π0 (arXiv 2410.24164) set this shape (3 B-parameter PaliGemma VLM + 300 M flow-matching expert, 50 Hz chunks); SmolVLA (arXiv 2506.01844) showed it survives aggressive shrinking (450 M total, consumer hardware, async inference). nanoVLA keeps the structure — prefix/suffix split, flow head, chunking, even the "expert reads the VLM through cross-attention" wiring — at another 50× down: trunk 2.6 M, expert 3.8 M, vision 0.4 M, text 0.7 M, total 7.5 M parameters.

Claims vs papers. The papers' numbers do not transfer to a toy — nothing here says "SmolVLA gets X%". What transfers is the shape of the claims: architecture solves visible tasks (c4 mirrors every VLA paper's headline), and what the inputs cannot carry bounds what the policy can do (c5/c6 are the course's own, measured here with Wilson intervals and verdicts instead of quoted). The same board grades the ForceVLA claims next lesson, scaled the same way.

Vision: one shared stem, 36 tokens per camera, camera + 2-D position embeddings
    def forward(self, *imgs: torch.Tensor) -> torch.Tensor:
        """imgs: one [B, 3, img, img] tensor per camera (static, wrist) -> [B, C*G^2, D]."""
        assert len(imgs) == self.cameras, f"expected {self.cameras} camera images"
        pos = self.pos_row[:, None, :] + self.pos_col[None, :, :]  # [G, G, D], 2-D position
        pos = pos.flatten(0, 1)  # [G*G, D], same order as the stem's flatten
        toks = [
            self.stem(img) + pos + self.cam_embed[c]  # [B, G*G, D] per camera
            for c, img in enumerate(imgs)
        ]
        return torch.cat(toks, dim=1)  # [B, C*G^2, D] = [B, 72, 256]
Text: 23 words, pad-masked everywhere — the mask arm C's blank text must not empty
    def forward(self, ids: torch.Tensor):
        """ids int64 [B, T] -> (tokens [B, T, D], key_mask [B, T] bool, attn [B, NH, T, T])."""
        key_mask = ids != PAD  # [B, T], True on real words
        x = self.embed(ids) + self.pos  # [B, T, D]
        x, attn = self.block(x, mask=key_mask[:, None, None, :])  # pads hidden as keys
        return x, key_mask, attn

Flow sampling: K Euler steps from noise to an action chunk (the suffix loop)
        x = torch.randn(shape, generator=generator, device=device)  # x at tau = 1 [B, H, A]
        xs = [x]
        dt = 1.0 / steps
        for i in range(steps):
            tau = torch.full((shape[0],), 1.0 - i * dt, device=device)  # [B], tau: 1 -> 1/K
            v = velocity(x, tau)  # [B, H, A]
            x = x - dt * v  # Euler step toward the data: [B, H, A]
            if trace:
                xs.append(x)
        return (x, torch.stack(xs)) if trace else x  # [B, H, A]; trace adds [K+1, B, H, A]

What to watch

All numbers below were measured in this image on the RTX 3080 and live in the lab's own run records (run 261031-220907-712a, its 100-episode eval and two follow-up probes on the same checkpoint). Training: 22.9 steps/s at batch 64 (clean box — the batcher is CPU-bound, a fully loaded box roughly halves it: the recorded 3×15 k suite took 86 min against ≈33 min at the clean rate), peak VRAM 0.87 GiB (torch.cuda.max_memory_allocated — it fits the shared 10 GB card ~11× over); the single-preset default 20 k is ≈15 min, inside the M1 "< 20 min" gate. The suite ran 15 k steps/arm (3×20 k would have crossed the evening budget; the shortened choice is on the record). The loss curve drops ~10× within the first 2 k steps and then grinds flat: arm A 0.66 → 0.064 at 2 k → 0.0078 at 15 k; arms B/C 0.44 → ~0.017 at 2 k → 0.0037 / 0.0034. On the single preset, the eval-points series (closed-loop success on 20 held-out seeds, every 5 k steps, drawn on the loss axis) is the honest curve: the loss looks finished long before the behaviour is.

The claims board — round 1, measured, and honestly red (100 episodes/arm, held-out seeds, eval seed 0; the CPU eval's numbers — a GPU re-run of identical seeds shifts a few episodes because the flow noise stream differs per device, e.g. T0 88 → 92, same verdicts):

  • c4 — nanovla_solves_visible: T0 88/100 (CI 80.2–93.0%) → NOT CONFIRMED, close but under the 95% gate. The misses: 10 force-aborts + 2 timeouts — with no force sense, a missed grasp becomes a 25 N dig-in, and the testbed scores that as failure by design (peak |F| mean 8.7 N, max 25.6 N). Levers before re-judging: the full 20 k schedule and more demos.
  • c5 — hidden_threshold_blinds_vla: T1 3/100 (CI 1.0–8.5%) against the 30–70% window → NOT CONFIRMED — on the LOW side, and the cause is sharper than the designed blindness. Verified in the canonical demos: lesson 15's T1 expert presses through the hybrid controller (Action(dp=np.zeros(3), f_target=ft) — on every press step the stored dp label is exactly 0 while f_target carries −(threshold + 0.8) N), so the descent lives in the controller's admittance, not in the action labels. A position-only policy has no press to clone: it approaches, hovers ~15 mm above the cap, and times out — 92/100 timeouts, a button touched in only 8/100 episodes, peak |F| mean 1.4 N. The flow loss is 0.0037 at the same time: imitation succeeded; the dataset simply does not contain the behaviour in dp space. The flagged fix (a plan-§8 gate decision, not silently done here): re-record T1 with a position-press expert (privileged stop on the click latch) — until then, lesson 17's position-head models hit the same wall.
  • c6 — language_is_load_bearing: T1 default 3/100 vs no-language 0/100, gap 3 pts (CI −2.7–8.5%) → NOT CONFIRMED — success cannot carry language when neither arm can press. A sharper probe (50 episodes/arm): arm B ends over a button in 50/50 episodes but closer to the instructed one in only 25/50 — chance — and blanked arm C in 28/50; B's final loss equals C's (0.0037 vs 0.0034), so the words bought no loss and the model never learned to read them. The suspect mechanism: the flow interpolant leaks the mode — at most τ the noisy chunk xτ already says which button this sample is drifting toward, so the velocity target is predictable without the text; and on these demos the wrong button merely times out (the press never happens), so nothing makes the tie expensive. The fixed T1 data should change both: a wrong-button press fails the episode.

In the trace panel, a T1 episode currently reads: approach, then a flat hover with Fz ≈ 0 until the timeout — scrub one and watch the subtask label stick at "approach". The mode strip is all grey: nanoVLA commands no wrench, the z-axis never leaves position mode. A T0 episode is the healthy contrast: reach, grasp, lift, SUCCESS. That grey strip — and the dp-zero press it cannot reproduce — is lessons 17 and 18's opening argument.

Exercises. (1) Interval honesty, again: run the suite eval at episodes = 20 — the intervals widen until the board turns indecisive; 100 episodes is what buys verdicts. (2) The data curve: train single on t0 with demos = 50 instead of 200 and compare the eval-points series — the loss curve barely changes (same objective) while closed-loop success drops; train loss measures imitation on-distribution, not recovery. (3) The deliberately wrong one: set n_exec = 16 (execute the whole chunk open-loop) and evaluate on t0. Measured on arm A's own weights — chunking knobs are inference-only, so this is exactly what retraining with that form value yields: 72/100 against the same-seed baseline 92/100, timeouts 1 → 14, aborts 7 → 14 — the model re-reads the world every 1.6 s, misses the grasp window, and corrections arrive a chunk late (lesson 4's trade, now with a price tag). The companion surprise: euler_steps = 1 degrades T0 barely (89/100) — lesson 2's result that a near-unimodal flow field is nearly straight, so one Euler step almost suffices when the scene has a single visible target; it is not a license for K = 1 where real multimodality lives.

Knobs

  • Preset: single trains one nanoVLA on Task's demos (with the eval-points series); claims_suite trains arms A (t0), B (t1) and C (t1, no language) sequentially into ONE checkpoint whose evaluation fills the whole M1 board.
  • Steps / Learning rate / Batch: the fit() schedule (warmup + cosine). 20 k × 3e-4 × 64 is the plan default; the loss is forgiving, the closed-loop success is what needs the tail.
  • Demos: first n episodes of the canonical set, in recording order — the data-efficiency knob (50/100/200/400 is the plan's curve; 400 needs a lesson-15 re-record at 400).
  • Chunk H / Executed steps: predict H, execute n_exec, re-plan. The reactivity trade from lesson 4; n_exec > chunk is refused.
  • Euler steps: inference-only integration count. 10 is the plan default; 1 is exercise 3.
  • Episodes / Eval seed / video (evaluate): held-out episodes per arm (claims want 100), the eval-seed window, and the rollout video (up to 4 episodes spread over the arms, static camera + force strip).

The single checkpoint is fit()'s house payload; the suite checkpoint holds all three arms labelled (kind "nfv-suite"), so one evaluate sees every arm — the pattern the lesson-17 ablation suites reuse. Round 1 leaves one flagged repair before lesson 17 can argue fairly: T1's demos must contain a position-space press (lesson 15's expert currently presses by wire — through the controller's force loop), or neither a blind nor a force-informed position-head policy has anything to clone. The grey mode strip stays the longer arc: lesson 17 feeds the wrench in (ForceVLA's FVLMoE among five entry routes), lesson 18 makes force an output — and the claims board stays the referee throughout.
