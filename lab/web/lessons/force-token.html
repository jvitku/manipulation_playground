The task

This is the headline result of the repo's Stage 1 (docs/FINDINGS.md, “Stage 1”), run here on the real simulator: a 3-axis gantry holds a square peg over a square hole with 0.5 mm clearance. The hole is at a random (x, y), σ = 1.5 mm per axis, and the policy is never told where. It only senses the peg: its position relative to the start, its velocity, and the wrist force/torque. The peg descends; if it lands on the rim it jams. To finish it must feel which way it is off, back up, shift 0.5 mm toward the hole, and try again. Success is 30 mm of insertion over the opening; >60 N aborts; 8 s is the time limit.

The data

A scripted expert that can read the hole position (privileged) descends at 5 cm/s, and on a jam retracts 3 mm and corrects one 0.5 mm step toward the true hole. At run start the lab collects the demonstrations on the gantry (the same loop as scripts/09_collect_expert.py; it takes about a second per five episodes) and slices them into windows of H = 10 past observations and the next K = 5 expert actions. Each observation has 18 numbers: position (3), velocity (3), compensated force and torque (6), a summary of the high-rate force over the interval (3) and the last action (3). The 9 force numbers are the force token. Demo action noise perturbs the executed action while collecting (labels stay the clean expert action). The default is 0, a replica of Stage 1, which used none; 0.5 mm is a robustness experiment (in a check run it lifted the no-force twin from 5/50 to 14/50 while the force policy stayed at 50/50, so the gap narrows).

The method: ACT-lite

A small action-chunking transformer. The H observation tokens go through a linear embedding plus learned positions and a 3-layer transformer encoder; K learned queries cross-attend to that memory in a 2-layer decoder and a linear head reads out K actions (about 800 k parameters; the MLP baseline flattens the window instead, 116 k). It is trained by plain regression. Near the end of an episode there are fewer than K future actions, so those slots are masked:

$$L = \frac{\sum_{b}\sum_{k} m_{b,k}\;\tfrac{1}{3}\left\lVert \hat a_{b,k} - a^{*}_{b,k}\right\rVert_1}{\sum_{b}\sum_{k} m_{b,k}}$$

where \(m\) is the validity mask, \(\hat a\) the predicted chunk and \(a^*\) the expert chunk, both z-scored per channel. Closed loop, the policy executes the first action of the chunk each step.

The zeroed twin. Feed the force channels off zeroes the 9 force channels in training and evaluation: same network, same data, no touch. At evaluation, Zero the force input blinds a force-trained model after the fact (the raw channels are set to 0 before normalisation). Compare the two at equal settings: the difference is the value of the force token.

Theory — why a force history resolves a hidden hole

The task is partially observed: the one variable the action should depend on — the peg-to-hole offset — is never in the observation. It leaks out only through contact. When the peg lands on the rim instead of in the hole, the contact point is off the peg's axis, so the wrist senses a torque whose sign says on which side the hole lies. One frame of that cue is small and sits inside an impact transient (the 3 summary channels exist because a spike can fall entirely between two 20 Hz control ticks); across a window of H frames the jam has a distinctive signature. That is why the force channels ride in every observation token rather than only the current one: the encoder's self-attention,

$$\operatorname{Attn}(Q,K,V)=\operatorname{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)V,$$

weights the frames around the jam by their content, wherever they fall in the window. The MLP twin reads each window slot through fixed weights, so “a jam, three steps ago” and “a jam, seven steps ago” are separate patterns it must learn one by one — it works, just worse (45/50 vs 50/50).

The loss says why the blind twin dithers instead of failing outright. L1 regression fits the conditional median, \(\hat a(o) \approx \operatorname{median}\, p(a^{*}\mid o)\), and without force a jam against a hole to the left and one to the right produce the same observation: the two expert corrections (±0.5 mm) are symmetric modes whose median sits uselessly between them. So the blind policy learns the unambiguous half of the recovery (retract) and not the ambiguous half (which way to shift); the force token's one sign bit is what makes the target unimodal. The chunk (K = 5) plays the same trick in time: predicting the whole retract–shift–descend program at once commits to one recovery instead of re-deciding — and re-averaging — every step.

The architecture

B = batch. The highlighted block is the 9 force channels (the force token); the dashed use_force switch is the zeroed twin. The loss node compares the chunk with the expert chunk, ignoring slots past the episode end.

ACT-lite: observation tokens in, learned queries cross-attend, actions out
def forward(self, x: torch.Tensor) -> torch.Tensor:  # x (B,H,obs) -> (B,K,act)
    h = self.norm_in(self.embed(x) + self.pos)
    mem = self.encoder(h)
    q = self.queries.expand(x.shape[0], -1, -1)
    out = self.decoder(q, mem)
    return self.head(out)
Stage-1 training step: masked L1 on action windows
def masked_l1(pred, y, m):
    return ((pred - y).abs().mean(-1) * m).sum() / m.sum()

What to watch: validation L1 is health, closed-loop success is the score

The charts show collection progress, then per epoch the train and validation masked L1 and the validation action error in mm. They do not show success: that is only measured by Evaluate, which flies unseen holes closed loop. The repo's own numbers (docs/FINDINGS.md, Stage 1; 50 fresh seeds, σ = 1.5 mm, 40 epochs) show why you need both:

README.md quotes the same headline as “50/50 vs 7/50” (a different run of the pictured episodes); FINDINGS.md’s 5/50 is the evaluated table above.

The validation loss separates the variants only 2× (0.07 vs 0.15), because 90 % of the steps are “descend 2.5 mm” and both fit them; the closed-loop gap is 10×. Without force the policy even learns the jam→retract reflex, but the same observation precedes “shift +x” and “shift −x”, an L1 regression averages them, and it retracts about 20 times per episode and dithers until timeout. The sign of the torque at the jam is the missing bit. And the arm is a warning that this is not a law of nature: on the Panda arm (docs/FINDINGS.md, arm result) the MLP with force reaches 39/50 against 4/50 zeroed, while ACT-lite plateaued at 4/50 with or without force — cause not isolated. Here only the gantry is wired in.

Knobs

  • Expert demonstrations: 50–500, default 300 (the Stage-1 size). Collection is cheap; the run time is mostly epochs.
  • Policy: act (history transformer) or mlp (flattened window). History H and chunk K default to the Stage-1 10 and 5.
  • Feed the force channels: off = the zeroed twin, trained blind.
  • Epochs (default 40) and learning rate (3e-4, cosine decay, AdamW, batch 256). A checkpoint is kept at each validation improvement and at the end.
  • Demo action noise (default 0 = Stage-1 replica; 0.5 = robustness experiment): see “The data”. Evaluation: Hole offset sigma (trained at 1.5; the repo reports that the force policy still reaches 50/50 at 3 mm), Clearance, Zero the force input, Episodes to render (the video concatenates them, each behind a short title card) and the eval seed.
