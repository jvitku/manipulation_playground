The task

The same Panda arm hidden-hole insertion as the repo's Stage 1 behaviour cloning, but now with no expert and no labels. The agent sees the last 4 steps of a 20-number observation (tip position and velocity relative to the start, the wrist force/torque, the last action), moves the peg tip by at most ±1 mm in x, y and ±3 mm in z per step, and gets a reward. The hole is shifted by a random σ = 1.5 mm and the policy is never told where.

The reward may look at the true hole (the policy never does): depth progress in mm, a potential-based shaping term that rewards getting closer to the hole in xy (it provably does not change the optimal policy), minus a time cost every step, minus a force penalty above 20 N, +50 for success and −20 for a 60 N abort, all times 0.1. Timeouts are not terminal: the critic still bootstraps through them. The first steps of a run are uniformly random actions; after that the actor acts with Gaussian exploration noise, one gradient update per environment step.

The method: TD3

Red = where a loss is computed; blue = how a network is updated. The three tricks that make it TD3 are all in the target: the minimum over two critics, noise on the target action, and the actor and target networks moving slowly.

Where each loss is computed

For a batch of transitions \((o, a, r, o', d)\) (\(d=1\) only for a real terminal, not a timeout), the target action carries clipped noise and the target is the smaller of the two target critics:

$$a' = \operatorname{clip}\!\big(\pi'(o') + \operatorname{clip}(\epsilon,-0.5,0.5),\,-1,\,1\big),\quad \epsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,0.2^2)$$

$$y = r + \gamma\,(1-d)\,\min\big(Q_1'(o',a'),\,Q_2'(o',a')\big)$$

$$L_{\text{critic}} = \big(Q_1(o,a)-y\big)^2 + \big(Q_2(o,a)-y\big)^2$$

γ = 0.99. Both heads regress to the same \(y\); taking the minimum fights the over-estimation a single critic suffers when the actor climbs its errors. This is the critic_loss chart; q1_mean and target_mean are the batch means of \(Q_1\) and \(y\).

Every second critic step the actor is updated, using the first critic only, and then both target networks are blended toward the online ones:

$$L_{\text{actor}} = -\,\tfrac1B\sum_b Q_1\big(o_b,\pi(o_b)\big),\qquad \theta'\leftarrow(1-\tau)\,\theta'+\tau\,\theta,\ \ \tau=0.005$$

The delay lets the critic settle before the actor chases it. The actor has no label at all: its only teacher is the gradient of the critic with respect to the action.

The story: a seed lottery

The repo ran this exact setup (150k steps, five seeds per condition, best checkpoint, 50 unseen holes; README.md, “Reinforcement learning”):

The same algorithm, the same code, only the random seed differs, and one run solves the task (every hole over 1 mm away inserted, in a median of 31 steps against the scripted expert’s ~114) while four others do not. The failed runs get stuck in local optima: diving fast into the 60 N abort, or hovering until the timeout. Convergence is bimodal: a run either finds the insertion behaviour or it never does, and no average over a few seeds describes it. That is the honest verdict of the repo too: force/torque is what makes the task learnable, but at this budget TD3 finds it in only 1 of 5 seeds, and behaviour cloning from the expert is far more reliable.

A single run here can look like either mode. Run seed 3 with force and you may think TD3 is easy; run seed 2 and you may think it is broken. Neither conclusion is justified by one seed. To compare two settings you need several seeds each, which is hours of simulation.

What to watch

  • A rising reward is not success. The reward includes depth progress and shaping, so a policy can collect a lot of it by hovering just above the hole or by diving to the abort. The Success rate chart (eval on held-out holes, and the share of the last 50 training episodes) is the score; the reward curve is a hint.
  • Bimodal convergence. Success sits near zero for a long time and then, in a lucky run, jumps. A flat success curve at 100k steps is what 4 of the repo’s 5 seeds looked like.
  • Q estimate vs target. q1_mean and target_mean should move together. If \(Q_1\) runs far above the targets, the actor is exploiting critic errors (over-estimation); if both explode, the critic has diverged and the reward curve will soon follow. A critic_loss that grows steadily, rather than spiking and falling back, says the same.
  • Actor loss is −Q, so it falls as Q rises. It tells you about the critic’s scale, not about how good the policy is.

Knobs

  • Environment steps: 20k–300k, default 100k. This is the cost: the simulation, not the networks, is the bottleneck, so the estimate shown before the run is long. Pause keeps the replay buffer (it lives in memory); Stop saves a checkpoint and ends the run.
  • Seed: the lottery ticket. Exploration noise (0.2), force penalty, time cost, hole-shaping weight change the reward and the search.
  • Feed the force channels: off = the zeroed twin, trained blind. Evaluate every: a held-out evaluation (20 holes) and a checkpoint at each; the best by eval success and the latest are kept.
  • Evaluation: unseen holes, hole-offset σ, Zero the force input (blinds a force-trained actor after the fact), episodes to render, eval seed.
