The question

Lessons 16 and 17 gave the policy eyes, words and a force input — and on the button it bought nothing. Here the task itself asks for a force: wipe a plate whose height and tilt are hidden, at 3 N (“gently”) or 8 N (“firmly”). A position command cannot hold a force on an unknown surface (lesson 15 measured it). The question is whether making force an output — ForceVLA2’s move — is what closes that gap.

The task

T2 from lesson 15: approach the plate, make contact, wipe a strip; success = ≥ 90 % of the strip covered and the normal force within ±1.5 N of the commanded target for ≥ 80 % of contact time, no abort, inside 15 s. The scripted expert wipes through the hybrid controller — which means its demonstrations carry the descent to the plate in the force target, not in Δp (this turns out to matter a lot; see What to watch).

The method

Fusion. State and force become queries into the trunk’s prefix instead of extra tokens:

$$E'_{state} = \operatorname{CrossAttn}\big(Q=[\varphi_P(p);\varphi_F(f)],\; K=V=E\big),\qquad E_{cond} = [E;\,E'_{state};\,\varphi_F(f)]$$

The last term is the bypass: raw force reaches the experts whatever the attention learned. A token-wise gate blends three experts densely and the result replaces the prefix as the action expert’s conditioning:

$$w = \operatorname{softmax}(W_g E_{cond}) \in \mathbb{R}^{S+3,\,3},\qquad E_{MoE} = \textstyle\sum_{m\in\{V,S,F\}} w_m \odot \mathrm{Expert}_m(E_{cond})$$

Hybrid head. Every chunk step is \(a_t = [\Delta p;\,g;\,f_t;\,s]\in\mathbb{R}^{11}\), flow-matched jointly exactly as in lesson 16. Controller (the paper does not publish one; this course’s choice, from lesson 15): per axis \(S_i = \mathbb{1}[|f_{t,i}| > 0.5\,\mathrm{N}]\); selected axes move by admittance \(\dot x_i = K_a (f_{t,i} - f_i)\) clamped to 5 cm/s, the rest follow \(\Delta p\). Prompt loop. The predicted progress \(s\) drives a subtask machine whose current word is appended to the instruction: advance at \(s \ge 0.8\), re-arm below 0.3.

Motivation — why each technique is in this experiment

The wipe task (T2). It is the one task where success is defined by a commanded force, so it isolates what a force output buys; the button and peg tasks are decided by force timing, which lessons 17 and 19 study.

Three arms in one suite. A = lesson 17’s fvlmoe with a position head (ForceVLA on this task); B = full ForceVLA2; C = ForceVLA2’s inputs and prompt with a position head — the comparison the paper never ran, and the one that isolates the output. Budget-reduced from the plan’s seven-row build-up chain (8k steps per arm on a saturated host).

Queries, not tokens. Appending a force token (lesson 17) let the model ignore it; asking the scene a question with it forces the force to pick what to read.

The bypass. Without it force would reach the experts only through whatever the attention learned to look at.

Dense gate. A blend rather than ForceVLA’s capacity-capped top-1 switch — no tokens are silently dropped (lesson 17 measured 55–67 % drops at training time).

Hindsight progress. The paper’s progress label could not drive its own subtask machine (tech-development 18.1); a label that restarts per subtask can.

Theory — force control is a choice of which variable to command

A stiff position controller pressing on a surface makes the contact force equal to stiffness × penetration: with an unknown surface height the force is whatever the height error makes it. Hybrid force/position control (Raibert & Craig) splits the task space: along constrained directions command a force, along free ones a position, chosen by a selection matrix. ForceVLA2’s contribution is to let the policy emit both targets and, implicitly through the force value, the selection — so the same network can approach in position and wipe in force. The force input tells the policy what is happening; only a force output lets it decide what should happen at the contact.

def update(self, s: float) -> bool:
    """Feed one predicted progress value; returns True iff the prompt advanced."""
    s = float(s)
    if not self.armed:
        if s < self.rearm_below:
            self.armed = True  # the new subtask's progress has visibly restarted
        return False
    if s >= self.advance_at and not self.is_last:
        self.index += 1
        self.armed = False
        return True
    return False

Read the 99-point gap carefully. A and C never touch the plate. The wipe expert descends by commanding a force (its engage step is dp = 0 with a force target), so position labels contain no descent and a position-head clone hovers. On this task the force output carries the motion to the surface as well as the force on it — the gap measures both. Gate tilt. The force expert’s weight averaged over token groups rises from 0.19 in free space to 0.29 in contact — but the rise comes from the vision and text tokens (0.06→0.27, 0.13→0.34); the force token’s own force-expert weight goes slightly down (0.44→0.39). The board reports the average; the breakdown is the lesson.

Tech development — what broke, how it was found, what changed

Every change that was needed to make this lesson's experiments and claims work, in the order it was found: symptom, how it was found, cause, change, take-away. Master copy: docs/force_vla_school_techdev.md.

18.1 — the paper's progress label cannot drive its own subtask machine. Symptom: fed the experts' eq.-10 progress traces, the machine advanced on step 1 of every episode. Found by: driving the machine with the label over 4 experts × 4 seeds and comparing to the expert's subtask per step — agreement 0.15–0.61. Cause: eq. 10 restarts at every expert phase, not every subtask, and its free-space factor exp(−2l) already reads 0.82 at l = 0.1 m, so the label sits near the 0.8 threshold from the start. Change: a hindsight label, k/(n−1) within each run of one subtask label (served by the dataset as s_sub). Take-away: a progress label must restart at the same granularity the prompt switches on — test it by driving the machine with it before training on it.

18.2 — distance-based progress fails too. The linear phase progress (1 − l/l₀) never left "approach" on the wipe and the peg tasks: force-settling and spiral-search phases do not shrink a distance. The hindsight label reaches 0.83–0.93 agreement on all four tasks; the residual gap is a deliberate switch at ~80 % of each subtask — early, never late.

18.3 — the prompt vocabulary listed words no demo carries. The wipe and insert phases run to the episode's end, so "done" is never labelled; the machine would have prompted a word absent from training. Change: prompts use only the subtasks the demos actually label. Take-away: the run-time prompt vocabulary must equal the training-label vocabulary.

18.4 — "exceeds 1" with a bounded s. The paper advances when s exceeds 1 while bounding s to [0, 1]. Change: advance at s ≥ 0.8, re-arm only after s < 0.3 — the hysteresis also stops a noisy s near the threshold from skipping subtasks inside one chunk. Known limit: a one-step subtask run cannot re-arm the machine.

18.5 — a residual would make the force bypass redundant. With a standard residual around the cross-attention, the force token reaches E'_state anyway. Change: no residual; tested — with the cross-attention zeroed, the state gradient is exactly 0 while all six wrench axes still reach the gate and experts. Consequence worth teaching: "experts see only state" still contains vision, because E'_state is a weighted sum of the prefix's values.

18.6 — the parameter budget differs from the plan. Measured +1.13 M (cross-attention 263 k, experts 789 k) against the plan's ≈ +1.7 M, which matches no d → 2d → d expert configuration. The lesson quotes the measured number.

18.7 — risk: sampling noise can switch an axis into force control in free space. The controller selects an axis at |f| > 0.5 N — 0.02 in model units — so residue on the f_target dimensions could flip an axis into admittance mid-air. The lesson-18 eval measures the spurious-selection rate before any deadband is added.

18.8 — a position head cannot even reach the plate. Both position-head arms scored 0/100 with zero contact steps (peak force ~0.5 N): they hover. Found by the eval's contact-step count, then reading the wipe expert: its engage step is dp = 0 with a force target, and its wipe steps keep dp_z = 0 — the descent to the surface exists only in f_target, executed by the admittance controller. A position-head clone learns dp_z ≈ 0. Consequence for reading the board: on the wipe task the force output also carries the motion to the surface, so the 99-point gap measures "can the policy reach the surface at all", not only force tracking. (Same family as entry 15.7 — what the demonstrator puts in which action channel decides what can be learned.)

18.9 — the force-tracking bar missed on "firmly". The full model wiped 99/100 but its contact RMSE was 1.155 N against the 1.0 N bar: 0.715 N for the 3 N instruction, 1.41 N for 8 N, with peaks up to 11 N — likely touchdown impacts at the higher target (not yet verified). The task's own success band (±1.5 N for 80 % of contact) passes; the plan's stricter gate does not. Bar kept; both numbers reported.

18.10 — the subtask prompt is load-bearing, measured. Freezing the prompt at "approach" for the whole episode dropped the full model from 99 % to 15/30 (firmly 2/14, gently 13/16) — the subtask word matters most for the 8 N instruction.

18.11 — spurious force-axis selection, measured. The risk from 18.7: x/y were selected for force control on 0.23 % of 2,144 free-space steps — no deadband needed. The z-selection rate in free space (87.7 %) mixes the legitimate force-controlled descent with any spurious selections and cannot be split with the current counters.

18.12 — the gate tilt is real, but not where expected. The force expert's weight rises from 0.19 (free space) to 0.29 (contact) averaged over token groups — claim confirmed — but the tilt comes from the vision and text tokens (0.06 → 0.27, 0.13 → 0.34); the force token's own force-expert weight goes slightly the other way (0.44 → 0.39). Report the breakdown, not just the average.

18.13 — budgeting on a saturated host. Training ran at 9 steps/s instead of the calibrated 25 (load ~115 on 24 cores), so the suite was cut to 8k steps per arm; the 100-episode eval ran as 8 parallel windows over one shared seed block and was merged (counts add exactly; RMSE and gate means re-weighted by contact steps and decisions). Windowed evals over a fixed seed block parallelise without changing the seeds.
