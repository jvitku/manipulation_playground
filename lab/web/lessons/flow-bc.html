The task

A peg must be inserted into a hole in a surface, seen from the side (x right, z up). The hole is 1.2 cm wide, 6 cm deep, and sits at a random x in [-8, 8] cm; the policy is told only a noisy estimate of it (σ = 3 cm by default), and the height of the surface (±3 cm) is not told at all. The peg is driven by an impedance controller: the action (a in [-1, 1]²) moves a reference point by up to 1 cm per step, the peg follows the reference until the surface blocks it, and then the controller's spring presses with \(F = k\,(\text{pos} - \text{ref})\), where the stiffness \(k\) (200–1000 N per unit) is drawn per episode and is unobservable without the force sensor. Push the reference deeper into the surface and the force rises; above 15 N a protective stop aborts the episode. The hole is surrounded by a 45° chamfer 1.2 cm wide, on which the reaction force tilts toward the hole: Fx tells which way to slide. The estimate is wrong by more than the chamfer catches (1.8 cm) in about half the episodes, so the peg must search by feel.

The data

A scripted expert: go above the estimate, descend until it feels contact, press lightly (3 N), slide along Fx on the chamfer, otherwise sweep a widening zig-zag across the surface, and push in once the peg drops. At the start of every run the demonstrations are collected (the Demonstrations knob), and only the successful episodes are kept for training; the run's status line says how many survived. Each observation has 22 numbers: the peg's position (x relative to the estimate, and z; 2 numbers) and the last 10 force readings (Fx, Fz each; 20 numbers). The force is noisy (sensor noise 0.05 N by default).

The method

The same flow-matching recipe as the previous task: the expert chunk is noised along a straight line,

$$x_t = t\,e + (1 - t)\,x_0$$

and the net regresses the velocity,

$$L = \left\lVert v - (e - x_0) \right\rVert^2$$

Sampling integrates \(K\) Euler steps from noise, \(x \leftarrow x - \tfrac{1}{K}\,v(x, t)\). What is new is the input: contact. The policy plays the first n_exec actions of each sampled chunk and then re-plans from the freshest force history, so the force window is what lets it notice that the peg touched down, that it is sliding down a chamfer, or that it is pressing too hard.

The zeroed twin. Feed the force history off replaces the 20 force channels with zeros (training and evaluation), so the net still gets the position but is blind to contact. Compare the two runs at equal settings: that difference is the value of touch. At evaluation, Zero the force input blinds a force-trained net after the fact.

Theory — a generative policy in a partially observed loop

The insertion task is partially observed: surface height and spring stiffness are drawn per episode and appear in no observation, and the hole is told only through a noisy estimate. Position alone therefore cannot distinguish hovering above the surface from pressing at 14 N. The force window is the stand-in for a belief state: touchdown is a step in \(F_z\), stiffness is how fast force grows per unit of commanded reference (\(F = k\,(\text{pos} - \text{ref})\)), and the chamfer shows as a lateral \(F_x\) bias pointing at the hole. The zeroed twin is the controlled experiment this design enables — identical architecture, parameters, data and optimiser, with only the information deleted — so whatever gap remains is the value of touch, not of capacity (TA-VLA's zero_torque twin, reduced to an MLP).

The policy itself is Diffusion Policy's receding-horizon recipe with a flow head: model the distribution of the next \(H\) actions given the freshest observation,

$$a_{t:t+H-1} \sim p_\theta\!\left(\cdot \mid o_t\right), \qquad o_t = \left(x_t - \hat{x}_{\text{hole}},\; z_t,\; F_{t-9:t}\right) \in \mathbb{R}^{22},$$

play the first n_exec of them, then re-sample. The chunk is for commitment: one sample sweeps one way instead of dithering between directions at every step. Partial execution is for reactivity: the force history can only change the behaviour at a re-plan, so n_exec is the feedback delay to contact events. The two knobs set a commitment-versus-reactivity trade-off, which is why both are exposed; pi0 and SmolVLA run this same loop at scale. Training only on the expert's successful episodes keeps the cloned distribution clean, at the usual BC price: the policy never sees recoveries from the failures that were filtered out.

The architecture

B = batch, H = action chunk. The highlighted block is the 20-dimensional force history; the dashed use_force switch is the zeroed twin. The loss node compares the net's velocity with the line's velocity, exactly as in the previous task.

Observation: state plus the force-history window
def _obs_tensor(raw: dict, dev, use_force: bool) -> torch.Tensor:
    """env obs dict (state 2 + torque 20) -> (n, 22); torque zeroed when `use_force` is false."""
    tq = raw["torque"] if use_force else np.zeros_like(raw["torque"])
    return torch.as_tensor(np.concatenate([raw["state"], tq], 1), dtype=torch.float32, device=dev)
Flow-matching loss: regress the velocity of a straight line
def loss(self, model, x0: torch.Tensor, cond, mask: torch.Tensor | None = None):
    # ...
    e = torch.randn_like(x0)
    t = self.sample_t(x0.shape[0], x0.device)
    tt = t[:, None, None]
    x_t = tt * e + (1 - tt) * x0
    v, extra = model(x_t, t, cond)
    err = (v - (e - x0)).pow(2).mean(-1)  # (B, H)
    # ...
    return err.mean(1), extra

What to watch: loss is health, closed-loop success is the metric

Two charts, and they disagree on purpose. The flow loss is an average over all noise levels, and most of that average is made of noisy levels that are impossible to get right, so the curve drops fast and then flattens early (this repo's train.py states it plainly: the loss of a generative head is a poor progress signal because it flattens early while closed-loop success keeps improving). So every eval every steps the run also rolls the current EMA model out closed loop on 200 fresh scenes, and plots the success. Read the loss as a health check — it is falling, no NaN, train and val together — and the success curve as the score.

The tutorial journal (docs/ta_tutorial/report_data.json, TAVLAMini with a force token, 400 demos, 3 seeds, lr 3e-4) shows both halves honestly. The loss falls from 0.185 at 500 steps to 0.130 at 3000 and then only creeps down (0.094 at 15k) while success goes 77% (1.5k) → 86% (3k) and then stays within noise at 85–87% to 15k: the late loss improvement bought nothing measurable. Neither curve alone could tell you that. With the force blanked, the same pipeline reaches only 59–65% and its loss ends higher (0.155) — but a loss of 0.155 does not say “61% success”, only the closed-loop run does. This task uses the plain MLPDenoiser, so expect the same shape but not the same numbers: measure, do not assume.

The reference, from the tutorial (docs/ta_tutorial.md, 400 demos, 3 seeds): force 86.7% vs zeroed 61.3%; at 100 demos 80.7% vs 62.7%, at 1500 demos 85.3% vs 63.5%. The failures differ in kind: without force the policy fails mostly by force abort (74–80 of 200 episodes against 22–24 with force), because it cannot feel when the peg has touched down and keeps pushing. Compare the force abort curve in the success chart, and the outcome colours in the viewer: green success, red force abort, amber wrong hole, grey timeout. The strip chart under the side view shows the Fx/Fz the policy was reading; the dashed lines are the 15 N limit.

Knobs

  • Demonstrations: expert episodes collected at run start; only successful ones are used. 100 already give most of the force benefit; more mainly raises the plateau slowly.
  • Training steps and Closed-loop eval every N steps: how long to train and how often to roll out the 200-scene evaluation (each one costs seconds, so a small interval slows the run).
  • Learning rate, Batch size: as before; the default is 1e-3 (the tutorial's T3 runs used 3e-4).
  • Action chunk / Actions executed per chunk: how far ahead the net plans and how many actions are played before the force history is read again (clamped to the chunk). Playing fewer keeps the policy reactive to contact.
  • Feed the force history: off = the zeroed twin (position only).
  • Sampling steps K: Euler steps per chunk; cost grows linearly. At evaluation: Hole-estimate error (how wrong the told hole position is), Force sensor noise, and Zero the force input.
