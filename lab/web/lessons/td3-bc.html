The problem with plain TD3

The previous lesson ended on a seed lottery: the same TD3 code solved the hidden-hole insertion in 1 of 5 seeds and failed in the other four, stuck in a dive-and-abort optimum (pushing down fast into the 60 N abort) or a hover optimum (waiting for the timeout). The repo’s Stage 1.5 (“R1–R4”) asked what it takes to make that reliable. The recipe it found is the one this task runs: put expert demonstrations in the replay buffer, and keep an imitation term in the actor loss. It is TD3+BC (Fujimoto & Gu, 2021) with one repo-specific detail that turned out to matter.

The recipe

  1. Demonstrations. At the start, the scripted privileged expert (the one that knows the hole position) inserts the peg n_demos = 100 times through the very same RL environment, so the transitions carry the real observations and the real reward. They go into the replay buffer and into a separate demo buffer. The agent never sees the privileged hole position: only the expert did.
  2. A critic that learns from them. The critic update is unchanged TD3 (twin critics, target smoothing, delayed actor). Because the demos are in the replay buffer, it sees what a successful insertion is worth from step 1 instead of waiting for random exploration to find one.
  3. An imitation term in the actor loss. Every actor update draws a fresh batch of \((o_d, a_d)\) from the demo buffer:

$$L_{\text{actor}} = q_w\cdot\frac{-\,\overline{Q_1(o,\pi(o))}}{\max\big(\overline{|Q_1|},\,1\big)} \;+\; \lambda(t)\,\overline{\big\|\pi(o_d)-a_d\big\|^2}$$

The Q term is normalised by the batch mean of \(|Q|\), so \(\lambda\) has a meaning that does not depend on the reward scale. The floor of 1 in the denominator is a repo fix (journal “R1-fix”): early on \(|Q|\) is near zero and dividing by it blows the Q term up into noise. \(q_w\) = 1 in training.

The decay λ(t), and why it must not reach zero

$$\lambda(t)=\lambda_{\text{floor}}+(\lambda_{\text{start}}-\lambda_{\text{floor}})\,\max\!\Big(0,\;1-\frac{t}{T_{\text{decay}}}\Big),\qquad \lambda_{\text{start}}=2.5,\ \lambda_{\text{floor}}=1.0$$

The imitation weight starts strong, so the actor first becomes the expert on the states the expert visited, then gives the critic more say as the agent explores. You can see it live in the Imitation chart (bc_weight, with the imitation error bc_loss).

The first attempt (R1) decayed the weight all the way to 0 over 100k steps. Three seeds climbed to 35–40 % evaluation success by 30–70k steps and then collapsed to 0 at 80–100k, exactly when the weight reached 0: with nothing anchoring it, the actor fell back into dive-and-abort. Keeping a floor of 1.0 (R1b) fixed that, and is the whole difference between a mean of 38.8 % and 58.4 % on the same five seeds.

The “asymmetric critic” feeds the critic the true hole position that the actor never sees. Most of these ideas are standard remedies; on this task they did nothing or made it worse. What worked was the unglamorous one: demonstrations plus an imitation term that never switches off.

Holes more than 1 mm off-centre: 58 % inserted with force against 7 % without; a one-sided permutation test on the per-seed means gives p = 0.0014. So the sensor decides it, and the recipe makes learning from reward far more reliable than the 25.6 % / 1-in-5 baseline.

But it is still bimodal. The repo’s own goal (80 % or better in 4 of 5 force seeds) was not met: only half the seeds become reliable, and the rest collapse early into dive-and-abort. One run in this lab lands in one of those modes (the table above has seeds scoring 5/50 next to 50/50), so a single seed tells you almost nothing about whether a change helped; the 62.2 % needs ten. Also note the lab default is 100k steps and a decay over 66k, a shorter run than the repo’s 150k / 100k (same ratio). And for this task, behaviour cloning with the right regularisation reaches 50/50: RL is here to learn the recipe, not to beat the imitator.

What to watch

  • Demonstrations collected first (about a minute at the default). Then the Imitation chart: bc_loss should be small and \(\lambda\) falls from 2.5 to 1.0 and stays there.
  • Success rate: with the demos it should leave zero early; a collapse later (the R1 pattern) is the sign that the imitation anchor is too weak.
  • Actor loss now includes the imitation term, so it no longer simply falls as Q rises.

Knobs

  • Expert demonstrations: 100 as in R4. BC weight at the start / floor / decay: 2.5, 1.0, 66k. Set the floor to 0 to reproduce R1’s collapse; set both the start and the floor to 0 for plain TD3 with demos in the buffer only (the start must be at least the floor, so the weight only ever decays).
  • The rest is task 7 (td3): environment steps, seed, exploration noise, reward terms, Feed the force channels (off = the zeroed twin), evaluation, video.
