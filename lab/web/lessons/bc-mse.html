The task

Two small problems, trained back to back in one run. T1 is one step: an obstacle sits at position \(c \in [-0.4,\, 0.4]\); the policy must output a 2-D waypoint that gets past it. T2 is closed loop: a point mass starts near (0, -1) and must reach a goal near (0, 1) without touching a disc of radius 0.3 near the origin; the action is a velocity in [-1, 1]² (0.04 per step) and the policy emits a chunk of horizon future actions at a time.

The data

The expert is scripted and bimodal. In T1 it passes the obstacle on the left or the right with equal probability: \(a = (c \pm 0.5,\ 0.5) + \text{noise}\). In T2 it picks a side at random per episode and holds it. Both sides are equally good, so both are in the data; the Expert noise knob sets the Gaussian jitter on every demonstrated action.

The method

Behaviour cloning is supervised regression: given the observation, predict the expert's action chunk. The loss, with the padding mask for chunks that run past the end of an episode:

$$L = \frac{\sum_h m_h \cdot \operatorname{mean}_d\!\left[(\hat a_{h,d} - a^*_{h,d})^2\right]}{\sum_h m_h}$$

where \(m_h\) is the padding mask, \(\hat a\) the predicted chunk and \(a^*\) the expert chunk.

The network is the same one the later tasks use, but it is fed zeros instead of a noisy chunk and a zero time embedding, so it cannot do anything except map the observation to one answer. Minimising squared error then makes that answer the conditional mean of the expert's actions. With two equally likely modes at \(c - 0.5\) and \(c + 0.5\), the mean is \(c\): straight into the obstacle.

The architecture

B = batch, H = action chunk (1 in T1, Action chunk in T2), 2 = action dims. The loss node sits where the network output meets the dataset's expert chunk; nothing else trains the net. Later tasks replace the zeros with a noisy chunk and the time embedding with the real one — the rest stays.

What to watch

Both losses fall smoothly and converge, and that is the trap: a low loss says nothing about whether the policy can do the job. Watch T1 collision. In this repo's tutorial T1 run (docs/ta_tutorial.md, 20k steps, 3 seeds) the MSE head put 100% of its samples inside the obstacle while a flow head put 3.0% there. The scatter below shows why: the predictions cluster on the line \(a_x = c\), between the two expert modes at \(c \pm 0.5\), inside the shaded band. In closed loop (T2) the effect is milder, since the mean of two half-chunks is partially corrected on the next step (tutorial T2, MSE with H = 16: 87% success vs DDPM 67.5%). Do not assume the two numbers move together.

Knobs

  • Demonstrations: how many expert episodes are collected. More demos shrink the noise in the mean but cannot split it into modes.
  • Training steps: optimiser steps per part (T1 and T2 each get this many). Loss keeps improving long after the behaviour stops changing — compare the curves with collision.
  • Learning rate: step size of AdamW with warmup and cosine decay. Too high and the loss spikes; too low and the run is undertrained at the step budget.
  • Batch size: samples per gradient step. Mostly changes loss noise and speed, not the mode-averaging.
  • Action chunk (T2): how many future actions the net predicts at once. Longer chunks commit to a side for longer but make the averaged chunk bend further into the obstacle.
  • Expert noise: jitter on demonstrated actions. Higher noise blurs the data and widens the prediction error without changing the fact that the mean lies between the modes.
