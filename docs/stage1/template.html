Two of the three goals were met outright and one partly. Getting there took two debugging passes that changed the task design, and those are the main lessons.

G1 · Panda PegInHole · hidden hole

The arm, on video

The Panda carries a 19 mm cylindrical peg on its flange above a square hole with 0.5 mm clearance. Each episode the hole moves by a hidden N(0, 1.5 mm) offset, and the peg starts above where the hole would be. The policy outputs where the peg tip should go next; a fixed low-level layer keeps the peg vertical and closes the arm controller's ~1 mm steady-state error. Four controllers run on the same hole: an untrained MLP, the MLP trained without force, the MLP trained with force (the best arm policy), and the privileged expert.

debugging pass 1

Lesson 1: where the torque is measured

The first arm run failed in a telling way: force made no difference (ACT-lite 4/50 with and without). The expert's decision rule needs one thing from the sensor, namely which side of the tip the rim is on. On the gantry the lateral force at the jam carries that (97 %). On the arm it doesn't. At the jams there is a large lateral force along the arm's reach (|Fx| ≈ 8.1 N vs |Fy| ≈ 0.15 N) whose sign is unrelated to the hole. The sensor sits at the flange, 100 mm above the tip, so that force leaks into the torque:

T_y(flange) = −0.100 m · F_x − dx_contact · F_z corr(T_y, −0.1·F_x) = 0.83

The useful term, where on the tip face the rim pushes, is buried. Taking the torque about the tip instead (T_tip = T_site − (p_tip − p_site) × F, the standard tool-centre-point convention) removes the lever arm. Freezing the xy integrator during contact did not remove the 8 N, so it isn't integrator wind-up; its source is still open.

Express the wrench at the tool centre point. With the flange torque, the x direction was a coin flip for the network; with the tip torque, the MLP goes from 13/50 to 39/50.

Arm results

G1 is met by the MLP (+70 points from force), not by ACT-lite. ACT-lite settles on the no-force solution: train and validation loss sit at ≈ 0.105 from epoch 50 to 250, whatever the history length (10, 16, 20), learning rate or epoch budget. On the gantry the ranking was the reverse (ACT-lite 50/50, MLP 43/50). The cause isn't isolated.
G2 · gantry · 50 unseen seeds per cell

Physics robustness

Stage 0 (M3) warned that timestep and contact stiffness (solref) change peak force about 2× without changing the motion. The policy doesn't care. Every cell of the grid is 100 % for the nominal policy, because it decides from the control-rate compensated wrench, which M3 found nearly solver-invariant (≤ 0.2 N main effects).

Beyond the plan: controller stiffness

M3 and M5 name the controller's stiffness kp as the knob that moves the observable force most. Varying the gantry spring at nominal solver settings finds the policy's real limit: it drops to 84 % at kp 200 and 78 % at kp 3200. Randomising kp in training, the obvious remedy, made it much worse. That is Lesson 2.

debugging pass 2

Lesson 2: pulses vs positions

With kp randomised, every policy collapsed to about 10 %. The diagnosis, step by step:

  • The cue is intact: the lateral force at the jam predicts the correction direction 93 % / 89 % (97 % / 90 % at nominal kp).
  • The model gets the direction right (80–83 %) but predicts the correction at 1 % of its size, in every kp band. An MLP does the same.
  • The expert makes each 0.5 mm correction in one control step at the end of its retract, and how long the retract takes depends on kp (2–10 steps). The L1-optimal prediction for a pulse whose timing is uncertain is zero.
  • Controls: giving the policy log(kp) doesn't help; the same code path with kp pinned to 800 reproduces the nominal data bit-for-bit (val 0.071); correcting at the jam step instead collapses too (the jam step isn't sharp either). The nominal result only worked because at fixed kp the retract always took 3–4 steps.

The fix changes what the policy outputs. With action_mode="xy_abs" the lateral action is a position relative to the episode start (z stays a delta), so a correction is a persistent step rather than a pulse:

A partial fix. With xy_abs, the kp-randomised policy predicts corrections at full size (validation 0.086 vs 0.188) and closed-loop success rises from ~10 % to 54–92 %, but it costs precision at nominal settings (80–92 % vs 100 % for delta actions). The untested hypothesis: per-step regression noise of ~0.1–0.2 mm on a lateral target, against 0.5 mm clearance. A lateral action that is both timing-robust and precise is the open problem (ACT-style temporal ensembling, or a discrete "correct now" output).

HDF5 export

scripts/15_export_hdf5.py writes the robomimic layout with h5py only. The named observation groups reassemble the policy observation bit-exactly (tested), the physics-rate wrench is kept grouped per control step, and the train/valid masks are the same episode split training uses.

data/                         attrs: total, task_spec, env_args (JSON: task params + obs group slices)
  demo_<i>/                   attrs: num_samples, seed, success, hole_xy_m
    actions        (T, 3)     policy actions
    rewards, dones (T,)       1 on the last step (reward only on success)
    obs/rel_pos, vel, ft_comp[_world], ft_hf_summary, prev_action, [tilt]   policy observation groups
    obs/s5_ee_pos, s5_ft_comp, s5_contact_wrench, ...                      PLAN §5 control-rate arrays
    obs/ft_raw_hf  (T, n_sub, 6)                                           physics-rate raw wrench
mask/train, mask/valid        demo names
