1 · The model: a VLM prefix, an action expert, a torque token

pi0 and SmolVLA process one attention sequence with two sets of weights. The prefix holds the observation (image patches, instruction, robot state) and runs through the vision-language model. The suffix holds an action chunk, H future actions with noise mixed in, and runs through a smaller action expert. In every layer, each stream computes its own queries, keys and values. Attention is joint under a block mask: prefix tokens see only the prefix; suffix tokens see the prefix and the whole suffix.

TA-VLA adds one token built from the recent force/torque history. Its ablation found the token works best in the decoder, next to the actions being denoised. An optional auxiliary head predicts the future torques from the expert's action outputs.

2 · One training step, one inference loop

Training (flow matching)

  1. Sample a batch of steps from the demonstrations: observation, torque history (z-scored), and the expert's next H actions x0. Positions past the end of the episode are padded and masked out.
  2. Draw a noise level t ~ Beta(1.5, 1) (more weight on noisy t) and noise e ~ N(0, I).
  3. Mix along a straight line: x_t = t·e + (1 − t)·x0.
  4. The network sees x_t, t and the observation, and predicts a velocity v. The target is the line's velocity u = e − x0.
  5. Loss |v − u|², plus 0.1·|aux − future torque|² with the auxiliary head. AdamW, warm-up then cosine decay, gradient clip 1, EMA of the weights for evaluation.

Inference (closed loop)

  1. Encode the observation once, then cache each layer's prefix keys and values.
  2. Start from pure noise x ← e at t = 1.
  3. Take K Euler steps, x ← x − (1/K)·v(x, t), down to t = 0. Only the expert runs at each step.
  4. Execute the first n_exec actions of the chunk (half of it here), then observe and re-plan.
Why chunks: a sampled chunk commits to one mode for H steps, so the policy doesn't flip between "left" and "right". Executing only part of it keeps the loop reactive to contact.
# src/fvb/ta_tutorial/generative.py  (FlowMatching.loss, abridged)
t   = Beta(1.5, 1).sample(B)                    # noise level per sample
e   = randn_like(x0)                            # x0: (B, H, A) expert chunk
x_t = t*e + (1 - t)*x0                          # straight path data -> noise
v, extra = model(x_t, t, cond)                  # cond: image, tokens, state, torque
loss = ((v - (e - x0))**2).mean(-1) * mask      # padding masked
loss += aux_w * ((extra["aux"] - future_torque)**2).mean()   # TA-VLA auxiliary term

3 · T1 — why the head must be generative

The observation c is the obstacle's position. Every expert waypoint passes it either on the left, at c − 0.5, or on the right, at c + 0.5, with equal probability. Actions stay inside [−1, 1], the range DDPM's sampler clips to. The same small MLP is trained three ways: flow matching, DDPM (ε-prediction, cosine schedule, DDIM sampling), and plain MSE regression. All three use 20k steps at lr 1e-3 and 3 seeds.

4 · T2 — closed loop, chunks, data and steps

A point mass starts near (0, −1) and must reach a goal near (0, 1) around a disc of radius 0.3. In each episode the expert picks a side at random. The observation is 6-D, the action is a velocity, and the policy is a 4×512 MLP denoiser. Success is measured on the same 200 evaluation scenes for every run.

5 · T3 — where the torque token earns its place

This is a 2-D peg-in-hole task. The policy knows where the hole is only to within about 3 cm (σ). The hole is 1.2 cm wide with a 1.2 cm chamfer each side. The surface height (±3 cm) and the contact stiffness (200–1000 N per unit) are random, and only the force sensor sees them. An impedance controller presses with F = k·(pos − ref), and above 15 N the episode aborts. On the chamfer, the reaction force tilts toward the hole.

All arms share the same TAVLAMini: a state token in the prefix, 8-action chunks with 4 executed, and 15k steps. Only the torque token changes: zero (the twin with its input zeroed), prefix, suffix (TA-VLA), and aux (suffix plus future-force prediction).

6 · T4 — a mini VLA: image, language, force

There are four language-conditioned tasks in one scene, seen through a 32×32 side camera: insert into the red hole, insert into the blue hole, press gently (2 N held for 8 steps), and press firmly (6 N). There are 1600 demonstrations across the tasks and 30k training steps. Images are rendered on the GPU from the scene state as a batch is drawn, so camera noise doubles as augmentation. Inserting needs vision to choose the hole and force to finish the alignment. Pressing to a force under a 5× uncertain stiffness needs force.

7 · T5 — the same recipe on the MuJoCo insertion skill

SortInsertEnv holds an M16 bolt with a random offset in the hand above a 17 mm hole, using the Panda's joint-torque sensing and fingertip taxels. The demonstrations, recorded with the scripted expert, and the 50 evaluation set-ups are the same ones the BC-MLP experiment used. The state is 16-D, the torque history is 60-D (4 frames of τ_ext 7 plus a tactile summary of 8), and actions are 3-D pad motions.

8 · What happened with the real SmolVLA

The same torque token went into a fine-tuned SmolVLA (450M parameters, pretrained lerobot/smolvla_base) on the image-based insertion skill: 158 demonstrations, 10k steps, re-planning every 10 steps, and 100 held-out set-ups. The ACT rows feed the same history through ACT's environment-state token.
