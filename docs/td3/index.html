Task and agent

Task. Robosuite Panda, a 19 mm cylindrical peg on the flange, and a square hole with 0.5 mm clearance per side. Each episode the hole moves by a hidden offset (N(0, 1.5 mm) per axis, clipped at ±4 mm) and the peg starts 20 mm above where the hole would be without it. Success means 25 mm deep within 200 steps (10 s at 20 Hz); |F| > 60 N aborts the episode. It's exactly the task in the BC report, the same low-level layer included: it keeps the peg vertical and closes the arm's ~1 mm steady-state error.

Observation. The last 4 steps of the 20-channel arm observation, stacked (80 numbers): tip position relative to the start (3), velocity (3), peg tilt (2), compensated wrench in world axes with torque about the tip (6), physics-rate summary max |F|, max |T|, std |F| (3), and previous action (3). Everything is z-scored with fixed statistics from the expert dataset. The no-F/T agent gets the 9 force/torque channels set to zero in every frame, in both actor and critic.

Action. 3 numbers in [−1, 1] → peg-tip target move of ±1 mm in x and y and ±3 mm in z per step.

Reward (per step, × 0.1)

r = Δdepth [mm] progress into the hole + (0.99·Φ(s′) − Φ(s)), Φ = −2 · |tip − hole|ₓᵧ [mm] potential-based pull toward the true hole − 0.1 time cost − 0.05 · max(0, |F| − 20 N) contact-force penalty + 50 on success, − 20 on a force abort

The reward is privileged, since it knows where the hole is; the policy never observes it. Potential-based shaping doesn't change the optimal policy (Ng et al., 1999), so both agents face the same problem. Only the no-F/T agent lacks any sensor that says which way the hole is.

TD3 and the networks

TD3 (Fujimoto et al., 2018) is an off-policy actor-critic for continuous actions. It learns a deterministic actor with three fixes over DDPG:

  • Clipped double-Q: two critics; the target uses the smaller one, which curbs overestimation.
  • Delayed policy updates: the actor and the target networks update every 2nd critic step.
  • Target-policy smoothing: clipped noise on the target action, so the critic can't exploit sharp Q peaks.

Exploration: 5,000 uniform-random steps, then Gaussian noise (σ = 0.2) on the actor's output. Evaluation uses the actor without noise.

actor   π(o):   80 → 256 → ReLU → 256 → ReLU → 3 → tanh
critic  Q1(o,a): 83 → 256 → ReLU → 256 → ReLU → 1
critic  Q2(o,a): 83 → 256 → ReLU → 256 → ReLU → 1
targets π′, Q1′, Q2′: Polyak copies (τ = 0.005)

critic loss  Σᵢ (Qᵢ(o,a) − y)²

What it means

Force/torque makes the solution learnable; TD3 rarely finds it. One of five F/T seeds solves the task (49/50 unseen holes, every hole more than 1 mm away, in a median of 31 steps, about 3.7× faster than the scripted expert). No seed without F/T gets beyond chance: at most 9 % of holes more than 1 mm away. The mean is 25.6 % vs 8.8 %, but that gap comes almost entirely from the one seed that succeeded.

Where the other runs get stuck. Two local optima show up in the curves:

  • Dive and abort: descend fast, bank depth reward, then hit the 60 N limit. This produces the deep dips in training return and the high force-abort fraction.
  • Hover: stay above the rim until timeout (0 N peak force). This is safe but earns nothing.

The seed that escaped learned what the expert does, faster: touch the rim, read the lateral force and torque, shift, and drop in, all within about 4 mm of the start.

Compared with behaviour cloning. Imitating the scripted expert is far more reliable at the same task: MLP + F/T 39/50 vs 4/50 without, every time it's trained. RL's advantage is speed: the successful TD3 policy inserts in ~31 steps vs the expert's ~114, because nothing forces it to copy the expert's slow 10 mm/s approach.

A reward-hacking episode, and the fix. The first batch of these runs reported up to 96–100 % "success". Their paths went 130–150 mm sideways. The success check only tested depth below the hole's top face, and the walls stand on the table, so lowering the peg onto the table beside the hole block counted as inserted. TD3 found this in both conditions; the behaviour-cloning expert never leaves the hole, so it never triggered it. Success now also requires the tip to be over the opening, with regression tests on both tasks. The behaviour-cloning results re-evaluated identically, and every number on this page comes from the corrected re-run.

Next steps, if TD3 is pursued: more seeds or a longer budget; demonstrations in the replay buffer (TD3 + BC) to escape the local optima; and a critic that also sees the hole position (asymmetric actor-critic), which keeps the policy unprivileged.
